Engineering Update

A Customer Dashboard Worth Coming Back To

A rewards hub, a referral QR code with real names, honest account standing — and the first pieces of a retention loop: rebooking nudges and following a shop.

Date:  July 29, 2026 Status:  ✓ 4 commits shipped · 2 features complete, awaiting commit Focus:  Customer Experience · Referrals · Retention

Summary

The whole day sat on the customer side. Four things shipped: the marketplace's view controls now sit in one consistent place with a Grid/List toggle for shops; referrals gained a scannable QR code and show friends' real names instead of masked wallet addresses; a new Rewards hub explains the tier ladder and how to earn; and Account Standing became a real explanation rather than a single line. Two further features were then built out end-to-end but are not yet committed — a smart rebooking nudge and following a shop, the first thing on the platform that brings a customer back on its own, now complete with a Following view, follower counts and a profile-page follow button.

1 Marketplace — Consistent View Controls NEW

The Grid / Map / Favorites controls used to sit in a different place depending on which tab you were on, and the Shops tab had no layout choice at all.

  • View controls moved below the Services / Shops tabs, so both tabs present them in the same position.
  • The control row has a reserved height — switching tabs no longer shifts the content underneath it.
  • The Shops tab gained a Grid / List toggle with a compact list layout, matching what Services already offered.

2 Referrals — QR Code & Real Names NEW

Referrals were technically complete but socially awkward: the invite could only be shared as a link, and the list of people you'd referred showed masked wallet addresses — so you couldn't tell who had actually taken you up on it.

  • Scannable QR code of the referral link, so a customer can refer someone standing in front of them without typing anything.
  • Real friend names in the referred-friends list, alongside each referral's status and the reward it earned.
  • Backend: a new getCustomerReferralsWithNames() joins the referee's name, and /referrals/stats now returns the name-enriched list.
Why it matters: a referral program only compounds if sharing is frictionless and the result is visible. A QR code removes the friction; real names make the payoff legible.

3 Rewards Hub & Overview Cards NEW

Tier progress existed in the data but was never explained to the customer. It now has its own home.

  • New Rewards tab — the Bronze / Silver / Gold ladder, current progress toward the next tier, and a plain-language list of ways to earn.
  • "Your impact" card on the overview — the savings a customer has actually accumulated, rather than an abstract token balance.
  • Review nudge card — surfaces completed bookings that still need a review, which is how shops accumulate ratings at all.
  • Dashboard container widened and the overview's extra max-width dropped, so content lines up with the header instead of floating narrower than everything above it.
Bug found along the way: the Active Services card linked to tab=orders's predecessor, tab=bookings — a tab that no longer exists. Every click landed the customer on a blank page. Now routed correctly.

4 Account Standing, Explained NEW

"Good standing" was a single line of text with nothing behind it — no indication of what it meant, what it was worth, or how it could be lost.

  • A status hero, the concrete perks good standing unlocks, and a no-show policy explainer so the consequences are stated before they're applied.
  • Customer settings refactored into sectioned navigation with ?section= deep-linking, matching how shop settings already work — so any part of settings can now be linked to directly from elsewhere in the product.

Largest single-file change of the day: SettingsTab.tsx, 777 lines touched.

5 Smart Rebooking Nudge NEW

Most bookings on the platform are naturally recurring — grooming, cleaning, oil changes — but nothing ever asked the customer to come back. This card does.

  • Reads completed bookings and estimates a per-service cadence from its category — grooming ~4 weeks, pet care ~6 weeks, automotive ~3 months, repairs ~6 months — then surfaces the services that are due again.
  • Renders nothing when nothing is due, so it never becomes background noise.
  • Alongside it, appointment-history stats in Account Standing — total, completed, upcoming and missed — giving the standing panel something concrete to stand on.
Caught while finishing it: the cadence lookup was reading a field the order record doesn't have (category instead of serviceCategory), so every service silently fell back to the 60-day default and the entire per-category table was dead code. The nudge looked like it worked — it just ignored the category it was built around. Fixed.

6 Follow a Shop NEW

A customer can now follow a shop and be told when it publishes something new. This is the platform's first genuine re-engagement loop — the only mechanism that reaches back out to a customer without a transaction prompting it.

  • Database: a shop_favorites table (migration 258), distinct from the existing per-service favorites.
  • API: follow, unfollow, follow-status and followed-shops endpoints under /api/shops/follow, restricted to authenticated customers.
  • Notification: a new shop_new_service type delivered in-app, over websocket and as a native push — declared through the notification registry, so no delivery channel can be silently dropped. Deliberately not transactional, so customers can mute it in preferences.
  • Fan-out: when a shop creates a service, its followers are notified. The fan-out is fully detached — it can never slow down or fail service creation.
  • Follow button on shop cards in both grid and list views, with optimistic toggling and revert-on-failure.
  • "Following" view — a toggle on the Shops tab, the direct counterpart to Favorites on Services, filtering the marketplace to the shops a customer follows. Unfollowing from inside that view removes the shop from the list immediately, and its empty state routes back to browsing all shops.
  • Follower counts on shop cards and the shop profile, fetched for a whole grid in one batched request rather than one per card — and kept in sync locally as the visitor follows or unfollows.
  • Labeled follow button on the shop profile, alongside the follower count. Hidden in preview mode, so a shop looking at its own profile isn't offered a button to follow itself.
Applied. The shop_favorites table is live — schema, unique constraint and both indexes verified against the database. The migration was renumbered 253 → 255 along the way: 253 and 254 had already been claimed by work on other branches, so keeping the original number would have tripped the migration runner's collision check.

7 Login — Saved Profiles Being Skipped FIXED

If a wallet was left connected to the site after logout — which MetaMask does by default — opening the login modal would silently re-authenticate that wallet and skip straight past the saved-profiles picker. The customer never got to choose which account to sign in as.

  • The stray wallet is now disconnected when the login modal opens, so the picker owns the choice.
  • Scoped narrowly — login only, and only when saved accounts actually exist — so the normal connect flow and sign-up are untouched.

8 Notification Hardening & Test Coverage FIXED

The follow feature shipped with no automated tests, so a safety net was added to the existing notification-gateway suite — five cases pinning the shop_new_service contract. The first run failed, for a good reason.

The bug the tests found: push notification bodies are built from metadata only — they never receive the in-app message text. The new-service entry was written as message || "Check out …", so the first half was permanently empty and the fallback silently took over. The result: the in-app notification read "Peanut Repairs just added 'Full Groom'", while the push banner that actually reaches the customer's lock screen dropped the shop name entirely — on a notification whose whole purpose is getting someone to return to that shop. Now built from metadata, so both read the same.
  • Five tests covering: three-channel fan-out, push title and body construction, graceful degradation when names are missing, and — most importantly — that the type does not bypass user preferences. That last one guards the design decision that a marketing-style nudge must stay mutable; it's exactly the kind of flag a future edit could flip without anyone noticing.
  • 14/14 passing in the gateway suite.
Worth a follow-up: the same dead message || pattern exists in five other notification types — announcements and the four AI-overage billing events. Each has a sensible hardcoded fallback so nothing is broken, but every one of those push bodies is ignoring dynamic text a caller may believe it's passing. Left untouched here: rewriting other people's billing notifications isn't a change to make silently.

Bottom Line

The customer dashboard stopped being a balance readout and started being a place with reasons to return. Rewards are now explained rather than implied, referrals are shareable in person and legible afterwards, and account standing states its own rules. The two strategically interesting features are now complete end-to-end: rebooking nudges turn one-off bookings into recurring ones, and following a shop — with a Following view, follower counts and a profile-page button — is the first mechanism on the platform capable of bringing a customer back without a transaction to prompt it. Everything typechecks clean, and the shop_favorites table is applied and verified — the work is ready to commit.
